Research, NLP

  • PyTorch
  • Python
  • Self-supervised learning
  • Contrastive learning
  • PDF processing

Named-entity recognition usually needs a large hand-annotated corpus per domain, which for biology literature runs to tens of thousands of dollars and months of expert time. This work learns animal entities and their relationships directly from unlabelled PDFs using contrastive objectives and an aggressive filtering stage, and outperforms a BERT baseline on the same task.

  • No annotation budget. The model builds its own training signal from document structure and co-occurrence.
  • Strict filtering drops low-confidence candidates so precision stays high without a human in the loop.
  • Generalises because nothing in the pipeline is specific to animal names.

Research, interpretability

  • PyTorch
  • Transformers
  • Switch Transformers
  • Sparse autoencoders
  • Scaling laws

Sixteen configurations from 13.1M to 597.3M parameters, comparing how dense and sparsely-activated architectures scale, then sparse autoencoders to look at what the models learned. The two families scale with opposite-signed coefficients, and Switch-Large reached the best perplexity of the study.

best perplexity, Switch-Large
  • Architecture-dependent scaling. Coefficients of −0.0381 for dense and +0.0173 for Switch on the same data.
  • SAE-based superposition analysis with a scoring method that gives consistent sparsity patterns across sizes.
  • Practical output: guidelines for picking an architecture when compute is the constraint, plus an open framework to reproduce the runs.
